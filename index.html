Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 August 2026

altr is a travel log: you photograph places you visit, rate them, and see where other people have been on a map. This policy explains what the app collects, why, who else sees it, and how to get rid of it.

It covers the altr iOS app. Words like “we” and “us” mean the individual who runs altr; contact details are at the bottom. The rules for using altr — what may be posted, and what happens when someone breaks them — are a separate page: the terms of use.

What we collect

Only what the app needs to work, plus one thing you can switch on and off. There is no advertising SDK and no third-party analytics SDK in altr, and nothing about you is sent to another company to be analysed.

WhatWhy
Username and password To create and sign in to your account. The password is stored hashed, never in readable form. We do not ask for your email address, real name, phone number or date of birth.
Precise location To place a review on the map, group nearby reviews into hotspots, and sort the feed by how close things are to you. A review’s coordinates are stored with it and are visible to anyone who can see that review.
Photos One photo per review, taken with the camera or chosen from your photo library. It is published with the review.
What you post and do Ratings, written reviews, place names, comments, likes, follows, friend requests, saved spots and collections.
Direct messages Messages you send to other people, so they can be delivered and shown. They are not end-to-end encrypted, and are readable by us on the server.
How you use the app — only if you turn it on Off unless you switch it on. altr asks you once, and takes either answer as final; you can change it whenever you like under You → menu → Help improve altr. When it is on, we record when you open altr and which of the five tabs you look at. That is the whole list: six kinds of event, each stored as the event, your username and the time. No screen contents, no search terms, no places, no text you wrote. It never leaves altr, is never sold or shared, and is deleted after 90 days. Turning it off deletes what was already recorded.
A notification address — only if you allow notifications If you allow notifications, your device gives altr a push token — an address for this app on this phone, which the operating system issues and changes from time to time. We store it against your account so we can tell you about a message, a comment on your post, a friend request, or a decision on an appeal. It is passed to Expo, which delivers the notification, and goes nowhere else. It is deleted when you sign out, when you delete your account, and when your device tells us it has stopped working.
A check that the app is genuine When you create an account, altr asks Apple to confirm that this is a real copy of the app on a real device, so that accounts cannot be created in bulk by software pretending to be altr. We store an identifier for a security key your device generates, together with a counter that stops the same proof being reused. The key itself never leaves your device — Apple's hardware does not let it out, and we never see it. What we store is not linked to your account, is not shared with anyone, and cannot be used to identify you or to see anything you do in the app. It is kept for as long as the app is installed on that device, because deleting it would stop that device from being able to create an account again.
Reports and blocks If you report content or block someone, we record who reported or blocked whom and why, so a moderator can act on it.

Most of what altr holds is meant to be public. A review — its photo, rating, text, place and coordinates — is shown to other people on the map and in the feed. Post accordingly, and treat the location on a review as information you are publishing.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Location — used only while the app is open, to position a review and to sort the feed by distance. altr does not track your location in the background. If you decline, the app still works: you drop a pin on a map by hand when posting, and the distance filter is switched off.
  • Camera — to take the photo for a review. If you decline, you can pick an existing photo instead.
  • Photo library — only when you choose to pick an existing photo. altr reads the one image you select; it does not scan your library.

You can change any of these later in iOS Settings, and the app will keep working with them off.

What is stored on your device

Your sign-in token is kept in the iOS Keychain. Your feed preferences — search radius, category filters and which scope you are viewing — are stored in ordinary app storage so the app opens the way you left it. Both are removed when you delete the app.

Who else your information reaches

We do not sell your information, and we do not share it for advertising. altr is built on a small number of services that process data on our behalf:

ServiceWhat it receives
Convex Hosts the database and the backend. All account data, posts and messages are stored there.
Expo Delivers push notifications. It receives your device's notification address and the text of the notification itself — a name and a short line, never a photo, a location or anything else. Only if you have allowed notifications.
Cloudinary Stores and resizes review photos. Photos are served from Cloudinary to everyone who views a review.
Apple Maps Draws the map itself, as part of iOS. Apple’s own privacy policy applies to it.
OpenStreetMap Nominatim Turns a coordinate into a city name for the label on the map. It receives a deliberately coarse coordinate — rounded to roughly 11 km — not your exact position.
Overpass API Lists named places near you when you are posting, so you can pick one instead of typing it. It receives the coordinate you are posting from.

We may also disclose information if we are legally required to, or where it is necessary to investigate abuse of the service.

How long we keep it

Your account and what you posted are kept until you delete them. Reports are kept after they have been dealt with, so that repeated abuse can be recognised.

Usage events are kept for 90 days and then deleted automatically. If you switch the setting off, everything already recorded about how you used the app is deleted at that moment rather than waiting for the 90 days to run out.

Content hidden by a moderator is deleted 90 days after it was hidden, which is also the window in which an appeal can put it back.

The device check described above is kept indefinitely, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. It is what a device proves itself with, so deleting it would leave that device unable to create an account on altr again. It is not attached to your account, so deleting your account does not remove it — and it tells us nothing about you beyond the fact that some genuine copy of altr once ran on some device.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app: You → menu → Delete account. You will be asked to type your username to confirm, because it cannot be undone.

Deleting an account removes:

  • your reviews, and their photos from our image storage;
  • comments and likes other people left on your reviews;
  • comments and likes you left on other people’s reviews;
  • your follows, friendships, blocks, saved spots and collections;
  • your conversations and the messages in them;
  • your account and every signed-in session.

Backups and server logs may retain copies for a short period before they age out.

Children

altr is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will remove it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete the information we hold about you, and to object to how we use it. Deletion is available directly in the app; for anything else, write to us and we will respond.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes we will update the date at the top of this page, and note anything significant in the app.
